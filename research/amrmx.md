# AMRMX on the system pack

What the binary of NCSU's operating system, AMRMX, shows about the DPR-2, as read from M. Howard's dump of his system pack (MH25, `magtapes/howard.disk`). Read on 2026-11-16 with our encodings ([dpr2-encodings.md](dpr2-encodings.md)) and disassembler (`disassemble` in `js/sim/dpr2.js`). The pack is studied and cited, not copied: no listing or word of it is kept here. Addresses are octal; disk positions are word indexes into the image, in decimal.

## Where it is

- **The image.** `howard.disk` holds the pack's 102,336 words, each in four bytes, low byte first. A cylinder is 1,664 words (16 sectors of 104).
- **The resident monitor.** Cylinder 2 (words 3,328–4,991) holds core 73400–76577, and the start of cylinder 3 holds 76600–77677: the two reads AMLD0 makes before it jumps to 73400 (ncsu-tapes.md). The words from 74000 are those of M. Howard's message (MH26).
- **The symbol table.** Cylinder 4, from word 6,656: 624 pairs, each a value then a five-character name. They agree with the labels in M. Howard's message. A value with an upper half names an overlay symbol: the upper half is the overlay's sector (its low nine bits, for the monitor's commands), the lower half the address.
- **The overlays.** The commands and most handlers are loaded when needed. A call is a `JPSR OVLDR` followed by a word giving the overlay's sector and its load address: 72524 for the monitor's commands (START, LOADA, GOTO, SNAP, …), 70000 for the handlers (tape, disk, card reader, punch, the display). Sector times 104 gives the overlay's place on the pack; every overlay found this way has its routines' entry words where the symbol table puts them.
- No routine on the pack is called DSPLY.
- **Its words.** Among the monitor's texts on the pack are the date prompt `MO/DA/YR = ` and `DATE ERROR.`, the AMRMX71 manual's messages as the program types them. The simulator's monitor asks the date in these words (R9).

## What it settles

- **LS tests that AR's lower half is not zero, and a conditional transfer does not skip.** The Teletype handler turns a tab into a space with `MDXO'F 11`, then `LSXO` of a word holding 51, then `MDXO'F 40`. A tab leaves AR zero, so the transfer is not made and AR ends as 40, a space; any other character has 11, 51 and 40 XORed in (11 ⊕ 51 = 40), and comes out unchanged. This works only if the transfer is made when the lower half is not zero and the next instruction still runs. The carriage-return handler beside it tests in the same way. This is how v0.0.2 reads LS (R1).
- **IR as a destination runs the word as an instruction.** A subroutine's entry word holds the jump back, so `MDIR entry` returns, and the conditional `ANIR entry` and `LSIR entry` are a conditional return; the resident monitor uses them eight times. It also builds an instruction in AR and runs it with `ARIR'F`. The simulator already treats IR so.
- **'B then 'K rotates by 7.** The routine that turns an AMOS character into ASCII rotates a table word by 'B and then 'K once for each place, and keeps the low 7 bits. With 'B 1 and 'K 6, rotating left, the table gives the ASCII of all 64 AMOS characters but tab and carriage return, which are control codes; with 'K 15 it gives nothing sensible. This is how v0.0.1 reads 'B and 'K.
- **AN fits "AR negative".** `ANAR ZERO` clears AR when it is negative, before a test of its lower half; `MDAS'F'N 40` then `ANIR` returns when AR was below 40. It agrees with v0.0.2's reading, though no use found proves it.

## What it shows of the display

The handler overlay at sector 210 (INIDB, ISDCH, ISFAS, DSPWT, DSPL) puts the Teletype's text on the screen. Its display routine drives AVG1 itself, in the way PRM1 describes DSPLY, through the destinations the block diagram gives ([dpr2-block-diagram.md](dpr2-block-diagram.md)):

- destination 10, AVG1's mode and enable, is set with 'A and 'O masks, and STOPS clears its upper half to stop the display;
- destination 06, AVG1's command, is cleared by STOPS;
- XY (destination 5), AVG1's flags, and destination 07 (GDD1 on the diagram, our multiplexer MX) are written as each character is drawn; 07 takes full values such as 37777 and 21176, not only a 4-bit selection;
- the routine waits for the end-of-vector (77756) and end-of-list (77757) interrupts, setting their pivots as it goes.

STOPS, in the resident monitor, points the frame clock (77755), end-of-list and light pen (77760) pivots at an idle routine, and clears bits of IC. IC is the interrupt enable register: the Teletype and disk handlers set and clear its bits with 'O and 'A.

So at NCSU the display was run, at least for text, by a DPR-2 program, as R2 supposes. How the user programs' pictures were drawn (by the games' own code, or a routine like DSPLY in an overlay not yet read) is not known yet.

## Running it (2026-11-23)

With the resident monitor in core at 73400–77677, as AMLD0 leaves it, and trial models of the disk controller and the Teletype's interface written from what AMLD0's source and the monitor's code expect, AMRMX runs on the simulator's DPR-2 from 73400, reads three overlays from the pack, and puts out its banner and the date prompt: `AMOS/2 DISK MONITOR`, `(PACK 000000000)`, `GENERATION : 21 APRIL 1976`, `CONFIGURATION :`, `MO/DA/YR =`. The models are a study harness, not yet part of the simulator. What they had to be:

- **DMS2, the disk** (channel 3). AMRMX puts a command word in 77720 and starts the controller by setting IC bit 5; S4 bit 5 is the controller ready. The command's upper half names the operation in its 07000 bits: 03000 seeks home; 02000 seeks by the number of cylinders times 16 in the lower half, toward cylinder 0 when bit 0 of the upper half (40000) is set; 06000 reads from the sector in the lower half (cylinder × 16 + sector, from the start of the pack). 04000, 05000 and 07000 are the other entries of the driver's table, at 77002–77005 with 06000: before 04000 or 05000 the driver checks a protect bit and reports error 4 or 5, so both write; LISTV writes a sector with 04000. 77721 holds the transfer: the word count above (bits 0–12), and the address before the first word below. On completion the controller writes a status word to 77722 (0 when all is well; in the upper half, 10 is a seek error, AMLD0 says) and requests service through a pivot: 77723 a transfer's normal end, 77724 an error, 77725 a seek's end, 77726 the end of a cylinder; 77727 also has a routine. The overlays read at start are the pack's sectors 130 (to 71252), 320 (to 72524) and 210 (to 70000, the display handler), 682 words each.
- **The Teletype's output** (channel 5). The monitor sets pivot 77750 to a routine that sends one bit, 77751 to the routine that ends a character, and enables a bit clock with IC bit 29. Each bit's routine puts the character, shifted, in BR and executes OPTY, which sends BR[22]: eight bits, the lowest first, each on its own interrupt, the eighth through 77751. What times the bits is not documented; the harness uses 110 baud.
- **The program interrupt's pivot is 77743.** AMRMX keeps its task dispatcher there and queues work with PINT (R10). Before this the simulator's was a guess, 77770.
- **A priority service ends at its Jump Indirect.** The Model 5 manual says a Jump Subroutine service ends at "the first Jump Indirect instruction or successful conditional jump" (SRM5 DPR3 p. 8). AMRMX's interrupt routines and the tasks they queue take conditional jumps before returning; ended there, the next request is served inside them and an interrupted register is lost. AMRMX runs correctly only if the service ends at the Jump Indirect alone, and the simulator now does so (R10).
- **The Teletype's input** (channel 5 too). To receive, the monitor points 77750 at a routine that shifts the character right and takes the line's bit from S4[14] with `SKUA 1` into its bit 7, and sets IC bits 28 and 29; the eighth bit's routine, through 77751, stops the clock (it clears bits 28 and 29), keeps the character's low seven bits and queues its handling with PINT. With the line modeled so (`js/sim/ttyline.js`, R23), AMRMX takes a date typed at its prompt and answers with the weekday, which it works out itself. The pivot at 77742 is the console keyboard's (ANK2, channel 6): its routine reads a 7-bit code from S5[1–7]. AMRMX uses it instead of the Teletype when the word the loader leaves in BR at start says so (ANKFG); AMLD0 leaves it clear.
- **Where the console's text goes.** The monitor's output routine sends a character to the Teletype, unless the display handler is in the overlay area at 70000, in which case it hands the character to that handler, which draws it on the scope. At start the display handler is loaded, so the banner and the prompt go to the screen (below).

## Its display (2026-11-23)

With AVG1 and the array modeled at register level (`js/sim/avg1.js`, R22), the same harness puts AMRMX's banner and date prompt on the simulated scope, drawn by its own display handler, letters, digits and punctuation right. DISP2's source (MH25, the tripp tape) gave the protocol; the handler's code and what it sends gave the rest.

- **The text buffer.** The handler keeps the console's text as 7-bit ASCII, four characters a word, at bits 0–6, 7–13, 15–21 and 22–28: bit 14, the lower half's sign, is skipped. It packs a character with `ARLS` by 23, 16, 8 or 1 places, so the first one passes into the sign bit: ARLS shifts as NORM does, and the simulator's kept the sign until now (R6).
- **Each redraw.** The handler redraws the whole buffer each time round its wait loop, so the screen is refreshed by software. It resets the array and sets its scale for the text through D07 (IC bit 10 clear), then for each character sets the character's place (DX, and DY at a new line) and sends the character's first vector word by executing the end-of-vector pivot.
- **The font.** Each character is a short list of stroke words in the overlay, in DSPLY's table layout (X, end of list, Y, draw), at full scale: about 0.67 wide and 0.98 high, scaled down by the array. `DIVI 20` splits a character's code by 40, as the EAU divides (R6), and picks a control routine (codes below 40), or a table entry that gives the offset of its list. Digits and punctuation share words with the letters: their pivot carries 'K, so their lists are read rotated by 6. Zero has a slash.
- **No wait for the beam.** The handler does not wait for a character to be drawn before it sets up the next: it replaces the pivot about 200 µs after it started the last list, and a character's dozen vectors are done well within that.
- **Register numbers.** As the handler uses them: 05 the size (two sizes, 01000 and 00400), 15 and 16 the place, 20 0.96 (as PS it puts the left margin at the edge of the visible square). It also loads 00, 03 (0.25 or 0, toggled by a control character), 06 and 21 (0.4955); what those are is not known. LIFE loads 05, 06, 07, 11, 13 and 20 with 37777.

## LIFE (2026-11-24)

AMRMX does not yet take a statement on the simulator (below), so LIFE is loaded from its file on the pack directly, as START would load it, and started at its entry word: the simulator's loader, not AMRMX's (R24).

- **The file.** Volume 10, origin 90 (sector 634). A DUMP file is a run of records, each a word with the address in its upper half and the count in its lower, then that many words; a zero word ends it. LIFE's are: two words at 70730 (a descriptor, with a checksum), the program from 120 to 43260 in records of 512, its symbols at 71422, three words at 70732 (its name, then 00120 and 43232, its first and last addresses), the monitor's pointers at 76326, and one word at 73777. The games tape's copy is the same core image from 120 without the record words.
- **The entry.** 120 holds `JUMP 00120`, a subroutine's entry word: START calls the program with `JPSR 120`, and it returns through 120 to the monitor.
- **The board.** Core from 200 to 37777 is the board, 128 × 128 cells (the instructions' XLOC and YLOC run 0–127), each cell a word that is an instruction: a generation is computed by executing the board. A cell's `MDIR 42521` runs `MDIR'X'I 42520`, which steps a pointer to the next cell and executes it, through IR, 16,384 times in a chain. The real processor runs such a chain without limit, each word sent to IR being simply the next instruction; the simulator's processor called itself for each and ran out of stack, and since v0.0.5 runs the chain as a loop (CPU-12).
- **The keys.** LIFE reads the function keys from S5's upper half: 0 stops, 1 runs (it clears the jump that closes its idle loop, so the loop falls through to a generation and its display), 2 and 3 type the instructions for loading and saving a figure on the scope through AMRMX (`INPUT … PVV = INPUT PACK-VOLUME … XLOC = X COORDINATE OF CENTER OF FIGURE. (0–127)`, `NAME(NAME,PVV) … TO ABORT OPERATION TYPE: CANCEL`), 5, 6, 9 and 10 move a cursor, 7 sets the cell under it, 11 clears it, 15 (FNS 16, read with IC bit 9 clear) restores the monitor's clock pivot and returns; 14 also runs.
- **The display.** LIFE loads the array (registers 05, 06, 07, 11, 13 and 20 with 37777), sets the display clock's pivot to its frame routine and the end-of-list pivot to its own, and turns AVG1 on with the clock (D10 61400). Its frame routine counts clock ticks in the lower half of 42661 from −2 to 0, so it draws at every third tick, 40 frames a second; it draws the cursor (a seven-vector box) and then the live cells.
- **Where it stopped, until 2026-11-27.** LIFE drew its cursor once and never again: it draws a frame only while 42662 is negative, which its end-of-list routine sets with `ARMD'O 42662`; read as an OR, that ORs in the interrupted program's AR, almost always positive. Its frame counter is also started with `ARMD'O`. The cause was the processor's store with 'O (below).

## Stores with 'O (2026-11-27)

AMRMX's statement reader and LIFE's frame flag failed for one reason: what `ARMD'O` writes to memory. The manual says 'O ORs the source into the destination (DPR2SPI pp. 21–22), and the simulator did so. Read with every use of a store with 'O in the sources we have, it is something else: it sets the word to −0.

- **The sources say so.** On the tapes, every commented `ARMD'O` sets a flag: "SET BUSY FLAG (LOCK CLOCK ROUTINE)" and "SET FAST FLAG" in DISP2, "SET HIT FLAG"; "SET DISK FLAG" and, in DSKWT, the "DONE INSTRUCTION" a disk interrupt executes in whatever program it finds; in the FORTRAN compiler "SET NEGATIVE", "KILL OVERLAY BUFFER", "NULL VOLUME LENGTH", "SET END OF LABEL BUFFER", a buffer whose last word is written `-0` and named "END OF LABEL BUFF FLAG". The flags are tested for being negative, and some with `MDAR'N` and a test of the lower half, which needs exactly −0.
- **An OR cannot do it.** In DISP2's frame routine, `MDAR BUSYX; JPAN FASTC; ARMD'O BUSYX` sets the flag just after finding it not negative, with the flag itself in AR: an OR leaves it as it was. AMRMX's statement reader sets its count with `MDAR'F PROTF; ARMD 73723; ARMD'O 73724`: the count must be −0 for the first character to come from 77505, where the statement is copied, and an OR leaves 77504. LIFE's end-of-list routine ORs the interrupted program's AR. AMRMX's octal printer sets its "a digit was not zero" flag with the digit in AR, then tests the flag for negative.
- **With it, both work.** The simulator now writes −0 for a store with 'O (R1, CPU-13); to registers 'O is still an OR, as the display handler's D10 and the word-building `MDAR'O` need. AMRMX then takes statements from the Teletype: errors and prompts come on the Teletype (`PROGRAM NOT IN MEMORY`, `VOLUME 2 NOT DEFINED`), listings on the scope (`LISTV(10)!` lists volume 10's files with their types, dates, lengths and sectors). Its banner reads `(PACK 0)`, `(34/1*)` and `CONFIGURATION : 26520 10100 10034 00100`, where it read `(PACK 000000000)`, `(/*)` and nothing: the octal printer suppresses leading zeros only with the flag set so. LIFE, loaded directly, redraws every third clock tick, stopped or running.
- **The statements, as AMRMX takes them.** A statement ends with `!`, arguments in parentheses; a file is named bare (`LIFE`), its volume given as a second argument. `START(LIFE)!` looks in volume 2, the default, which this pack lacks; `START(LIFE,10)!` asks on the Teletype `REQUIRED: … NEW PACK/VOL =`, as if volume 10 were not mounted, and so does `LOAD(LIFE,10)!`. How a volume is mounted is not yet found; `LISTV` reads volume 10 without one.

## The array's registers, from four programs (2026-11-27)

With the stores fixed, LIFE redrew, but its board stood in the upper right quadrant, mostly off the screen. The programs on the pack that drive the array through D07 (AMRMX's text, LIFE, Space War, and the display package QUBIC and BROSE share) were read for what they put in each register, and Space War, started from its entry the same way (its symbol SPCWR, 1266), was run: it draws its two ships symmetric about the central star, among the stars, as it should, so positions are signed with 0 at the center.

- **A D07 word** names its register in the low five bits of its upper half and gives the value in its lower half; 100 resets the array first; 200 starts a run, in which the words that name no register load the next registers in turn. QUBIC's package loads registers 03–22 in one run of sixteen; LIFE places each cell with a run of two, X and Y; AMRMX's one run loads 06 and 07.
- **What the registers are.** All four set 06, 07 and 10 (or 06 and 07) to 1.0: the matrix's diagonal. 15, 16, 17 are the position (Space War sets each object's there). 05 is the size (AMRMX's two character sizes, Space War's small and large objects). 20 is the overall scale. LIFE alone sets 11 and 13 to 1.0, and sets z, D06's lower half, to −0.5: read as R13 and R23, the terms that carry z into x and y, they shift its board, whose cells are at 0 to +1.0, by −0.5, to the center. So read, LIFE's board is centered, a 128 × 128 grid of 0.08-inch cells.
- **Register 01** is the converter's input, one bit a channel: QUBIC and BROSE select 1, 2, 4, 10, 20, 40 in turn, the six dials, and LIFE selects 1 before reading S5. LIFE paces its generations by it: dial A from slow (a generation every 4 s or so) to fast.
- **LIFE plays.** Three cells set with keys 7 and 10 make a blinker; key 1 runs, and the blinker turns between a column and a row every generation.
- **QUBIC** started at its entry (222) stopped with the FORTRAN run-time error `OTS ERR #50 AT 00225, ARGUMENT ERROR`. *Settled on 2026-11-28:* two causes.
  - **AR at entry.** The main program calls 4EROR's setup (`JPSR 17553`), which returns AR as it found it, and then `JSLS 17616`, which reports the argument error if AR's lower half is not +0: a program entered with no arguments. Our loader now clears AR (R24).
  - **`JPSR` with destination 01.** QUBIC's dump carries the linking loader's symbol table (71422–72441), and every symbol's value there is a `JPSR` to its address with bit 5 set, `0102 0xxxxx`. The FORTRAN unit table holds such words (16551: `0102014144`, the reader in 4AINP), and 4IO copies one to 15735 and executes it. The simulator stopped there, "no such instruction"; it now ignores bits 2–5 in the special instructions other than the conditional transfers and the arithmetic unit's (R1, CPU-15).
  - With both, QUBIC plays on the Teletype: `TYPE YOUR MOVE`; a move as three digits, `111`; `YOUR MOVE IS 111`, `TYPE YES OR NO`; `YES`; `MY MOVE IS 444`.
  - **Its board** *(drawn since 2026-11-28; R22)*. QUBIC's descriptor names its units: QUBIC 120–1774, BOARD, GETC, CRUNC, BOX, MERIT, STNB, STRAT, GRAFX 10076–13230, and the FORTRAN library from 13231 (4GRFX, 4TTY, 4AINP, 4IOUT, 4DSEP, 4IINP, 4FTCH, 4CONV, 4IO, 4IEXI, 4IMPY, 4ERSS, 4EROP, 4EROR, to 17651). GRAFX drives AVG1 in a mode the model lacks: its lists are pairs, D06 a command in the upper half (0010 move, 0030 draw) and z in the lower, then D05 x and y as whole 15-bit halves (the board's lines at ±0.125 and ±0.375), sent by `MD06'X'I 77756` and `MDXY'X'I 77756` (10307). Each frame (its clock routine at 11630) ORs D10 bits 0, 1 and 2 on, keeps bits 5 and 14, sends D06 0010000000 and D05 0, and sets the end-of-list pivot to 10150, where the frame's drawing starts. On the model that word ended in an end-of-vector, not an end of list, so nothing was drawn. *Settled from GRAFX's own code:* with D10 bit 2, D06's command is 0100 move, 0200 draw, and a command with neither ends the list (the frame's second word, 12640, is 1; the board's lists end with such words); AVG1 asks for the words in turn through 77756 as a pointer, the command after each vector and the x and y after each command, as GRAFX's start-up code does for the first pair. D10 bit 14 is kept and does nothing in the model.
  - **The array's run.** QUBIC loads 03–22 in one run of sixteen, its matrix words stored with `ARMD'H`, so that their upper halves hold the products' low bits (`014:37773`, `010:37774`). Read as register names, they cut the run short and scattered the matrix. Turning each dial a quarter turn shows which registers change: the run is positional, and gives the matrix's layout (R22). AMRMX's run of two ends where it writes IC (`MDIC'A`) before two single writes built with rotations (`04021:17667`, `12020:36561`, registers 21 and 20); LIFE starts one run after another. Dial 3 sets 05, SC: centered, the board has no size.
  - **Playing it** (the Programs menu, since v0.0.7): moves are three digits from 1 to 4, typed on the Teletype and confirmed with YES; X and O appear in their cells; dials 0–2 turn the board, dial 3 sizes it, and the simulator sets them to show it when QUBIC starts (ours). Dials 4 and 5 change nothing seen.

## Space War's keys (2026-11-27)

Space War reads S5 twice a frame, as Adage's switch routines do (below): with IC bit 9 set, the sixteen keys' first fifteen into 01102, key n at bit 14 + n; with it clear, the pedals and the sixteenth key, S5 bits 12–14, into bits 12, 13 and 29. Each action loads the word complemented and tests one bit. Read so, and checked by holding each key and pedal on the simulator (keys numbered 0–15 as the strip numbers them, FNS 1–16):

- **Player 1:** 0 turns anticlockwise, 4 clockwise (its angle, 01045); 12 fires (its torpedo count); 8 hyperspace; **foot pedal 1** thrusts (fuel and velocity).
- **Player 2:** 15 turns anticlockwise, 11 clockwise (01046); 3 fires; 7 hyperspace; **foot pedal 2** thrusts.
- **The game:** 1 restarts. 5, 6, 9 and 10 each toggle a setting when pressed: 5 draws the border of the playing area, 9 hides the stars, 10 turns gravity off (the ships' velocities stop changing), 6 decides what the star does to a ship that falls in, destroyed (as at start) or held there (02321–02336). 6 and 10 also set IC bits 0 and 1, which light the panel's IC 0 and IC 1. 2, 13 and 14 do nothing.

The players' keys are the box's outer columns, 0, 4, 8, 12 and 3, 7, 11, 15, with a pedal each for thrust.

## The function keys and IC bit 9 (2026-11-27)

S5's upper half has fifteen bits for sixteen keys and two pedals. The tripp library's commented routines show how they fit (MH25): FSRR, the function switch read routine (E.R.T., revision D, January 1971), sets IC bit 9 and reads S5 as FNS 1–15 ("SET BIT FOR FNS(1-15)", "MASK FNS 3-15": FNS k is bit k − 1), then clears it and reads FNS 16 at bit 14 ("AND IN FNS(16)"); FPBS, the foot pedal bit sense routine, clears IC bit 9 and finds pedal 1 at bit 13 and pedal 2 at bit 12. The FORTRAN compiler's listing control samples FNS 1–15 the same way. Since v0.0.5 the simulator does so (R18); until then it put the sixteen keys on S5 by number, ignored IC bit 9, and so could not read the sixteenth key or the pedals at all, and gave Space War's second read the keys 12–14 instead of the pedals. LIFE's return to AMRMX, tested with IC bit 9 clear, is the sixteenth key (15), not 14.

## Statements and files (2026-11-27)

What AMRMX's own statement macros (the pack's sector 640 on, written by NCSU's operators) and its loader show about running a program from the pack.

- **The syntax**, as the macros write it: a file's name in quotes, then its pack-volume, or a list of them, `START("AGTRT",30)`, `START(%1,(130,7))`. A pack-volume is one octal number, pack × 100 + volume (`LISTV` is itself a macro, `LISTV(%1/100,%1&77)`), so volume 10 of pack 0 is `10`. `SYST(10)!` makes volume 10 the system volume, which statements without one use; until then it is 2, which this pack lacks (`VOLUME 2 NOT DEFINED`).
- **START loads relocatable files.** START and RETRIEVE share the relocating loader (overlay 200 for the file search, sector 160 for the statements): it walks the volume's directory backwards and considers only entries of type 1, RELOC (the high four bits of the type field, as on the tapes: 1 RELOC, 7 ATEXT, 11 DUMP). `START("9SE",10)!` loads 9SE at 120 and runs it; RETRIEVE loads without starting. For a DUMP file it finds nothing and asks `REQUIRED: … NEW PACK/VOL =`.
- **What restores a DUMP file is not yet found.** LIFE, QUBIC, Space War and BROSE are DUMP files, core images from 120 on. No code in the system cylinders tests for type 11; `LOADA` is the 1971 manual's load from tape (`LOADA(FILE, TAPE)`) and fails on a disk volume; `REST`, `BEGIN` and the bare name answer `PROGRAM NOT IN MEMORY`. The statement may be a system program on a volume the dump lacks (20–37), or an operator's macro there. Until it is found, the simulator restores a DUMP file with its own loader and starts it at the symbol named after the file (LIFE 120, SPCWR 1266, QUBIC 222), as `Bare.startDump` does.
- **A restored dump's names are statements** *(2026-11-29)*. A dump carries the linking loader's symbol table (71422–72441), each entry a value and then a name, and AMRMX calls a name in it when it is typed as a statement, the arguments after the call. BROSE runs so (below). Whether `LIFE!` or `QUBIC!` starts those games the same way is not yet tried.

## BROSE

Read and run on 2026-11-29, from the games tape: the pack's copy ends where the dump does, the tape's is whole (34 records, core 120–34017 and the same blocks at 70730, 71422, 72422, 76326 and 73777 as QUBIC's).

- **What it is.** A FORTRAN subroutine of three arguments that draws a rose curve, with the same display package as QUBIC (GRAFX, 4GRFX) and FORTRAN's real arithmetic (4RADD, 4RMPY, 4SIN …). Its units: BROSE 120–23776, THING, GRAFX 24014–27146, 4GRFX, 4IO, 4SIN, 4AMOD, 4AINT, 4SERS, 4STOR, 4SMPY, 4RDIV, 4RMPY, 4RADD, 4RTOF, 4RTOI, 4ITOR, 4UTIL, 4ERSS, 4EROP, 4EROR (to 34016).
- **Its entry**, 23545, is the value before the name BROSE in the symbol table. It sets up 4EROR's error handling, checks that AR holds 3, the argument count (`MDXO'F 3`, then `JSLS` to the argument error), and executes the three words after the call (`MDIR'I`, `MDIR'X'I`) to get its arguments' addresses, which it keeps at 120–122. Typed as `BROSE(7,500,3)!`, AMRMX calls it so, with the numbers at 34026–34030.
- **The arguments.** The second is the number of points the curve is drawn with, held between 3 and 10000: 100 shows corners, 500 is smooth. The first and third shape the rose: (3,100,1) draws three petals over half a turn, (5,100,2) ten, (7,500,3) fourteen, (4,1000,9) eight. Exactly how they combine is not yet read from the code.
- **PAUSE.** With the rose drawn it prints `PAUSE` and waits, the picture refreshed: 4UTIL saves the PULSE 1 pivot (77776), puts `ARMD'O` of a flag there, and loops until the flag is −0. PULSE 1, at its usual setting `MDIR 77776`, executes that store; BROSE then ends and AMRMX takes statements again. So FORTRAN's PAUSE waited for PULSE 1, and the −0 store (R1) is what ends it.
- **When it is restored.** The dump restores the monitor's state as it was when BROSE was dumped (blocks at 71422–72441, 76326, 73777). Restored before AMRMX's own start is over, that state is overwritten: BROSE then computes its rose and pauses with AVG1 off, nothing on the screen. Restored after AMRMX has taken the date, as the operator's statement begins, the rose stays on the screen through the pause. The simulator restores it so (`Bare.then`).
- **No dials.** Its display package reads the dials only when the program asks; BROSE does not, and neither the dials, the function keys nor the pedals change its picture.
