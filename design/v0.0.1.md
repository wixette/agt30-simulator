# v0.0.1: the first pre-release

**Status:** approved 2026-11-09; built on branch `dev-v0.0.1`; released as v0.0.1, a pre-release, on 2026-11-10. Changes made while building are marked *(build)*.

v0.0.1 is a **pre-release**. It is small, it is honest about what it reconstructs, and it is good enough to show people who know the machine. It is public from the first commit, so nothing in it is there only to collect feedback (the reasons are in [../docs/ui-design.md](../docs/ui-design.md), U12).

## 1. What a visitor can do in v0.0.1

- **Look round the installation in 3D.** The operator's desk with the 21-inch scope and the console devices, the ASR-33 Teletype, four racks, two tape drives and two disk drives, and the cables between them. The scope shows the live picture. Clicking a part opens its card.
- **Move between four viewpoints:** Room, Console, Screen and Tube, with a viewpoint menu and animated camera flights.
- **Load one of seven programs** from the Programs menu. The menu *types* the program's AMOS statements on the Teletype, as an operator would have typed them, as fast as the monitor takes them; the printer echoes them at its own 10 characters a second (U8).
- **Work the console:** six dials, sixteen function keys, and the light pen on the Screen viewpoint.
- **Use the Teletype:** paper and keyboard, with the AMOS monitor answering statements typed in by hand.
- **Read a card for each program:** what it rebuilds, how to use it, what is ours, the statements behind it, and its ADEPT source.
- **Read in About** what the simulator reconstructs, with the version and the licences.

Desktop browsers only (U2). English only, with every text held as a message key (U11).

## 2. The programs

Each program is an ADEPT source file in `programs/`, plus an entry in `programs/index.json` (a manifest). Pictures that the paper's figures rebuild are in core when the terminal starts (R15). The statements are what the Programs menu types. *(build)* Each program's statements begin with `RESET!`, which returns the machine to its state at start (AMRMX p. 11), so every program starts clean.

| Id | Program | Rebuilds | Statements the menu types | Controls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `letter-a` | The letter A | FJCC68 Fig. 4: 13 vectors, 11 drawn | `RESET!` `DSPLY (V.A)!` | none (the Tube shows it drawn) |
| `cube` | A cube turned by the dials | Fig. 7 | `RESET!` `DSPLY (V.CUBE, 2)!` `CLOCK (DIALS+1, DI.EX)!` | dials 0–2 |
| `perspective-cube` | A cube in perspective *(build, after the manual check)* | FJCC68 p. 753: perspective done by the processor, since the array cannot divide | `RESET!` `ADEPT ("PCUBE")!` `DSPLY (V.PCUBE)!` `CLOCK (DIALS+1, DI.EX)!` `CLOCK (PCUBE+1, PC.EX)!` | dials 0–2; the work is shared: a CLOCK routine in DPR-2 code works out each corner's depth and scale (one divide a corner), the picture loads the scale before each point (LDSCL), and the array turns and scales it; about 3 ms of the processor's 25 a frame |
| `tunnel` | The tunnel, with depth cueing | Figs. 15–16 | `RESET!` `VIEW (.19, -.556, 37777)!` `DSPLY (V.TUND)!` `CLOCK (DIALS+1, DI.EX)!` | dial 0 flies in; the card's *Depth cueing* switch types `VIEW (,,0)!` or `VIEW (.19, -.556, 37777)!` |
| `town` | A town from one house | FJCC68 p. 749, structured images | `RESET!` `ADEPT ("HOUSE")!` `ADEPT ("TOWN")!` `DSPLY (V.TOWN)!` `CLOCK (DIALS+1, DI.EX)!` | dials 0–2 |
| `tic-tac-toe` | 3-D tic-tac-toe, 3 × 3 × 3 | After D. Ogden's 4 × 4 × 4 game at NCSU (NCSU) | `RESET!` `ADEPT ("TTT")!` `DSPLY (V.TTT)!` `CLOCK (DIALS+1, DI.EX)!` `TTT!` | keys 0–2 choose a layer; the light pen picks a cell; dials 0–2 turn the board |
| `random-lines` | 1,000 random lines | Fig. 12 | `RESET!` `DSPLY (V.RAND, 2, , BUMP)!` `CLOCK (DIALS+1, DI.EX)!` | dials 0–2; the card's *1,000*, *2,000* and *4,000 lines* type `OPEN (NLINES)!` and the number; *Back to 40 a second* types `DSPLY (, 2)!` |

*(build)* The tunnel sets its own VIEW, because the depth cue's FRONT and BACK belong to the tunnel's geometry, and the card's toggle then changes DIM alone. The town and the game add the CLOCK statement, since their dials turn the picture.

**Fig. 12 is option (b), decided 2026-11-09.** The picture holds up to 4,000 lines, and `NLINES` sets how many are drawn. At 1,000 lines it turns steadily at 40 frames a second. Past about 2,000 lines it no longer fits a frame *(build: measured; the design guessed 1,600)*. DSPLY then does what its BUMP option did: it raises RATE and types the new RATE on the Teletype (DSPLY p. 8). The visitor sees the flicker start, and the machine itself reports the rate. BUMP only ever raises RATE, so the card also offers *Back to 40 a second*. There is no "No array" switch, and no frame-rate readout of our own. *(build)* RANDN, a resident DPR-2 routine called by a JSR item at the top of V.RAND, moves the RET that ends the picture after NLINES lines.

**Every card action types a statement** (U8). The card never changes the machine directly. Anything the card can do, a visitor can also type, and the paper is a true record of the session.

## 3. Stages

These are internal stages of the work, not public milestones. There are no developer pages (the decision is recorded in [../docs/conventions.md](../docs/conventions.md)). Each stage ends with `pnpm test`, `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm check:refs` and `reuse lint` passing.

| Stage | Delivers |
|---|---|
| **S0. The repository** | `LICENSE`, `LICENSES/`, `REUSE.toml`, `package.json` (pnpm, Node 24), `tsconfig.json` (strict JSDoc), `.gitignore`, `.gitattributes`, `.editorconfig`, `.npmrc`. Tools: vendor (three.js, Roboto Flex, Material Symbols), stamp, check-refs, serve. CI: tests, REUSE, check:refs. Pages: deploys once the tests pass on `main`. |
| **S1. The display** | Ported with review: `words`, `image`, `array`, `vecgen`, `dsply`, `frameclock`, `terminal`, `font`, and the figures v0.0.1 needs. The opcode table is re-read from the DSPLY manual's scan, not taken from OCR. `research/data/agt30.json` holds the documented facts with their sources. *(build)* Only generated pictures are built in JavaScript (the font, RAND's lines, the tunnel's items); the letter A, the cube and the viewers are ADEPT files in `programs/`, assembled at start. |
| **S2. The terminal at work** | Ported with review: `dpr2` (with the `ENCODING` table), `adept`, `amos`, `amoscode`, `teletype` (the paper model), `agt`, the console's devices, and the pen-hit table. The programs move to `programs/*.adept` and the manifest. |
| **S3. The CRT** | The phosphor and the eye's integration window, the depth cue, the face texture for 3D, the full-resolution Screen canvas, and the light pen's hit test. The X, Y and Z traces for the Tube. |
| **S4. The installation in 3D** | `params/terminal.json`, the scene built from it, the viewer (lights that travel with the camera, picking, the live scope face, lamps, cables), the Tube cutaway and the beam. |
| **S5. The page** | The top bar, the stage with its viewpoints, viewpoint menu, cards and live inset, the console strip, the dock (Program and Teletype), the status line, About, `en.js` with its tests, and `?selftest`. |
| **S6. The release** | README (8800 shape), CHANGELOG, `pnpm shots`, a manual pass in Chrome, Safari and Firefox, the version set to 0.0.1 (`pnpm version`), a release PR, then the `v0.0.1` tag on its merge, marked as a pre-release on GitHub. |

**What "port with review" means for each module:**
1. Re-check every rule in it against the source it cites.
2. Rewrite its structure, names, types and comments to [../docs/conventions.md](../docs/conventions.md).
3. Write its tests fresh, from the documented facts, under the new acceptance IDs (section 4).
4. Copy no tests or checks from the earlier prototype. The behaviour it showed is the bar to meet, not code to keep.

## 4. Acceptance

The IDs are grouped by area, not by stage. Each statement is checked by one Node test (`it("DSP-3: …")`), and, where it makes sense in a page, by one `?selftest` check. The detailed specs live in [architecture.md](architecture.md) §6. Here is the outline:

| Area | Prefix | Checks, in outline |
|---|---|---|
| Words | `WRD` | One's complement (−0); octal input and output; fractions and angles as DSPLY defines them |
| Image format | `IMG` | Every item and data type decodes as the manual defines it; the letter A is 13 vectors, 11 drawn |
| Array | `ARR` | x' = PS[DX + SC(R x)] to within quantisation, for every row (FJCC68 eqs. 5–7) |
| Vector generator | `VG` | The AVG1 times; 1,660 3-inch vectors fill a 40-frame within a few per cent |
| DSPLY | `DSP` | RATE 1–5 give 60, 40, 30, 24 and 20 frames a second; ALL, CUT and BUMP; 16-level nesting; DIM's 10 : 1; a dial read at the tick shows in that frame |
| CRT | `CRT` | 40 frames a second looks steady, 20 flickers; the eye window is a whole number of frames; the pen reports the item that drew the line |
| Processor | `CPU` | Instruction times; JPSR linkage with arguments; RVCD reads a dial to 2 LSB in under 1 ms |
| Monitor | `MON` | Each statement the programs use; `?` for an unknown statement; OPEN and a typed value |
| Assembler | `ASM` | ADEPT's statement forms; undefined symbols reported by line |
| Teletype | `TTY` | 10 characters a second; CR and LF as separate actions; 72 columns |
| Programs | `PRG` | For each manifest entry: its statements, typed, give no `?` and reach the program's documented state (for example, TTT played through to a win). Fig. 12 at 1,000 lines runs at 40 frames a second, and at 4,000 BUMP raises RATE and types it. |
| Scene | `SCN` | Under 30,000 triangles; named nodes; no intersections across all pairs; everything supported; each cable joins its two parts; the face shows the CRT; the tube fits the cabinet |
| Views | `VW` | Each viewpoint is reachable by the viewpoint menu and by keyboard; Escape climbs one level; the inset shows outside Room and Console; the 3D view stops rendering under Screen |
| Page | `APP` | Every text is a message key; About lists every reconstruction in [reconstructions.md](reconstructions.md); the version stamps agree; no network request leaves the site |

**Not automated:** the look in real browsers, the feel of the dials and the flights, and whether a newcomer understands the program cards. These go in the manual pass in S6.
